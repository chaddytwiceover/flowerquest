# Game Vision

Flower Quest should become a polished, accessible, portrait-oriented 2D adventure game built around exploration, magical gardens, environmental puzzles, collectibles, abilities, enemy avoidance, secrets, and progression.

Core loop: Explore -> Discover -> Interact -> Solve -> Collect -> Unlock -> Progress.

The game should feel deliberately designed rather than like a collection of disconnected features.

## Scope Boundaries

Do not build, unless explicitly approved:

- Multiplayer
- Accounts or authentication-dependent gameplay
- Online leaderboards
- Microtransactions
- Currency or shops
- Crafting
- Procedural campaign generation
- Twenty additional levels
- 3D gameplay
- Complex inventory
- Cloud saves
- Social features
- Live-service systems

Finish the ten-garden adventure first.

## Campaign Target

| Level | Primary Design Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Movement and collecting |
| 2 | Patrol timing |
| 3 | Exploration and gates |
| 4 | Multiple objectives |
| 5 | Power Blooms |
| 6 | Moving/environmental hazards |
| 7 | Switch puzzles |
| 8 | Enemy awareness / stealth |
| 9 | Multi-stage adventure challenge |
| 10 | Finale combining learned mechanics |

Existing levels do not have to be rebuilt at once. Improve them incrementally.

## Story

Keep storytelling light. Prefer short NPC conversations, environmental clues, Monnie reactions, visual changes, small story events, and concise dialogue.

NPC dialogue should usually communicate at least one of: story, character, objective, hint, or worldbuilding.

## Monnie

Monnie should feel like the center of the game. Prioritize readable animations, responsive movement, reaction animations, emotional feedback, consistent sprite style, and good collision feel.

Potential reactions: collect, hurt, discover, win, surprised, low health, power-up, secret found.

Do not radically change Monnie's appearance without Product Owner approval.
